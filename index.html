How typicality is calculated

Typicality measures how far the compound sits from the centre of approved-drug property space, taking account of how the eight properties below vary together. It is the Mahalanobis distance computed over MW, cLogP, TPSA, H-bond donors and acceptors, rotatable bonds, aromatic rings and plane of best fit (PBF, a measure of 3D shape), with log scales for MW, TPSA and the counts, using the covariance of 1,294 FDA-approved drugs (ClinTox). The figure is the share of those drugs that lie further from the centre than this compound: 50% is as typical as the median drug, and below 5% is rarer than 19 in 20 approved drugs.

It has no fitted weights or desirability curves, so it describes what approved drugs look like rather than what a drug should look like. Approved drugs include injectables, antibiotics and natural products, so an atypical result is a prompt to look closer, not a verdict.

How this map is made

PC1 rises with polar surface area, H-bond donors and acceptors and size. PC2 rises with cLogP, size, aromatic rings, rotatable bonds and the plane of best fit, and falls with H-bond donors. The axes come from a principal component analysis of eight properties (seven from RDKit, plus the plane of best fit from a 3D conformer) over 1,294 FDA-approved drugs, so any new structure can be placed on the same map.

About these measures

Plane of best fit (Firth, Brown and Blagg 2012) and the normalised PMI ratios (Sauer and Schwarz 2003) describe 3D shape. Both come from one conformer (OpenChemLib, MMFF94s+), using heavy atoms; the reference drugs were treated the same way, so the comparison is like for like. One conformer is a snapshot: flexible molecules can adopt other shapes.

Bertz CT (Bertz 1981) grows with size, branching, multiple bonds and heteroatom variety. Fsp3 (Lovering et al. 2009) is the fraction of carbons that are sp3. Kier Φ (Kier 1989) indexes flexibility from atom counts and shape indices. PubChem complexity is PubChem's own Bertz/Hendrickson-type index, shown when the compound is in PubChem.
